01

Domain

Healing · Nature · Balance · Growth · Fertility · Cycle of Life · Protection For Nilera, healing is not closing a wound.

A body getting back on its feet, recovering its strength after illness, dried soil producing crops again, or a damaged ecosystem renewing itself over time are different forms of the same principle:

Life’s ability to repair itself. Nilera can accelerate, guide, and support this process. But she does not remove life’s natural limits. For this reason, her healing is not immortality. Trying to eliminate death entirely means disturbing the very cycle of life Nilera represents. Nilera’s task is not to keep everything alive forever;

it is to help what can live to be able to live again. “Healing does not mean continuing as though nothing happened.”

02

Mythology

Among the seven sisters, Nilera is one of those who encounters life’s fragility most directly. She sees wounded bodies, people who fall ill, lands that dry out, harvests that are lost, and nature’s struggle to get back on its feet. This is why Nilera’s compassion does not come from naivety. She is compassionate because she knows what pain is. Yet seeing her only as a gentle, protective figure would be a great mistake.

Nilera can be extremely resolute when necessary. Because healing does not always mean comforting. Cleaning a wound can hurt. To prevent a disease from spreading, one region may be separated from others. A plant’s rotten branches may be cut so that it can live. Sometimes, to save something, one must first stop what is causing harm. This is the difficult side of Nilera’s teaching:

Compassion is not always gentle.

03

Nilera’s Worldview

For Nilera, life is valuable. Yet being valuable does not mean it must be preserved unchanged. Bodies change. Soil changes. Rivers change. People age. Wounds close, but may leave scars. For this reason, in Nilera’s teaching, healing: Does not mean returning to one’s former state. A person can heal after a great loss. But they may not be the person they were before that loss.

A city can be rebuilt after a disaster. But it does not have to be exactly the same as the old one. A wound can close. But its scar may remain. For Nilera, this scar is not evidence of failure. It is the memory of having survived.

04

Her Bond with Serapha

Seen from afar, Serapha may look like a hot, arid country. Yet the kingdom’s life is built around water. The great lake and the seven rivers are like Serapha’s veins. Around the rivers, the land changes. Vegetation thickens. Palms and green spaces appear. Settlements, farmland, and roads follow the lines of life formed by water.

This is why, in Serapha, water is not merely a natural resource. It is the condition for life to continue. And this is precisely where Nilera’s influence on the kingdom can be seen. Her teaching does not tell the people of Serapha they must conquer nature. It tells them they must understand what nature needs.

05

The Seven Rivers

Serapha’s seven rivers connect the kingdom’s different regions. A significant share of its settlements has developed around these waterways. The rivers provide drinking water. They nourish the soil. They make farming possible. They make transporting people and goods easier. And they form distinctive habitats around them. For this reason, a river being polluted or its flow being disturbed is not merely an environmental problem.

It can affect thousands of people’s lives. This is why, in Nilera’s teaching, protecting water is more a social responsibility than a religious symbol. Because someone who uses a river’s water is not responsible only for themselves. The same water will also reach another settlement downstream. One of Serapha’s fundamental ideas emerges from this: Life is interconnected.

06

Healing

Nilera’s healing is not unlimited. She can close a wound. She can support the body’s healing. She can strengthen resistance to disease. But she cannot endlessly force the body to do something it cannot do at all. And most importantly: She cannot erase death. This boundary separates Nilera’s and Yuna’s domains. Nilera tries to protect what can continue living.

Yuna, when life ends, preserves that ending’s place within the natural order. Their powers come very close to one another. But they are not the same.

Nilera’s task begins where the question “Can you live a little longer?” exists. Yuna’s task, however, is sometimes where the question “Can we let you go now?” exists.

07

Healers

In Serapha, healing is not left solely to a sacred miracle. In Nilera’s teaching, knowledge is also part of healing. Plants, the structure of the body, wound care, clean water, nutrition, the symptoms of diseases, and the environment’s effects on health are learned and passed down through generations. A healer’s task is not merely to intervene when disease appears.

Preventing disease from appearing is healing, too. For this reason, protecting clean water sources, ensuring food safety, and improving people’s living conditions can be seen as extensions of Nilera’s teaching. In Nilera’s world, healing is not only magic. It is knowledge.

08

Nature and Fertility

Serapha’s fertile areas are not unlimited. For this reason, the relationship with the soil matters. Exhausting the soil to obtain the largest possible harvest in one year is not considered success in Nilera’s teaching.

Because fertility is not measured only by the question “How much did we produce today?” It is also measured by “Will anything still be able to grow here tomorrow?”

Although Nilera’s understanding of growth resembles Lena’s, there is an important difference between them. Lena concerns herself with the possibilities and transformation of growth. Nilera, with growth’s ability to be sustained. Lena wonders what a seed might become. Nilera looks at whether the soil is still alive so that it can grow.

09

Nilera in the Eyes of Her People

The people of Serapha turn to Nilera in life’s most vulnerable moments. When a child falls ill, when a person is severely wounded, when a prolonged drought begins, when a crop becomes diseased, when a river’s water diminishes, or when a family awaits a new life coming into the world, her name may be spoken. For this reason, Nilera is a goddess very close to her people.

Her presence is not felt only in great temples. In a healer’s hands, in a farmer’s soil, in the water a mother gives her child, in food offered to a stranger, traces of Nilera’s teaching can be seen. Because, for Nilera, protecting life does not always require great heroism. Sometimes: It is sharing what someone else needs in order to live.

10

The Boundary of Compassion

One of Nilera’s most difficult questions is this: Should everything be saved? At first glance, the answer may seem obvious. But nature does not work that way. If a disease is spreading, a plant carrying it may need to be destroyed. To protect life in one region, another area may be temporarily abandoned. When someone cannot be saved, a healer spending all their resources on them may cause other people who could have been saved to be lost.

Nilera does not like these decisions. But she does not avoid them. Because her compassion does not look only at the pain in front of her. It tries to see all the life surrounding that pain. For this reason, Nilera’s goodness is not passive. Sometimes, it demands terribly difficult choices.

11

Nilera Among Her Sisters

Among her sisters, Nilera is often the one who concerns herself with consequences. After an argument ends, after a war is over, after a decision is made, after a disaster has passed, she looks at what remains. Who was wounded? What was lost? What can be saved? What can grow again? For this reason, there is a strong contrast and complementarity between her and Dara. Dara stands in front of danger.

Nilera then helps those left behind back onto their feet. Lena wants life to grow. Nilera protects the conditions in which it can live. Yuna carries the great balance between life and death. Within that balance, Nilera fights on the side of life for as long as possible. Yet she also knows she must not interfere with Yuna’s domain when the time comes.

12

Character

Nilera is warm, protective, and patient. She does not belittle people’s pain. Yet she also does not think that everything someone in pain wants is good for them. This is why Nilera’s compassion can sometimes seem stern. She may keep someone who needs rest from working. She may tell someone who is exhausting themselves to stop helping. Even if she has the power to perform the miracle someone wants, she may refuse if she thinks it will cause greater harm.

Nilera’s greatest weakness is carrying other people’s pain as though it were her own responsibility. When she cannot save someone, she may struggle to accept that it is not always her own failure. Because, for someone who has devoted her life to healing, sometimes the hardest sentence to say is this: “That was all I could do.”

“Healing does not mean continuing as though nothing happened.”
