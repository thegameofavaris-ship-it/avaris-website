01

Domain

Truth · Justice · Compassion · Love · Secrets · Spiritual Understanding For Talia, truth is not merely the line between right and wrong.

A person’s words may be true and still fail to tell the whole truth. An action may be wrong, yet a judgment made without understanding what caused it may be incomplete.

For this reason, Talia’s power is not limited to punishing lies. She can see the intention behind words, the feelings people conceal, and the truth behind an event’s visible surface. Yet one of Talia’s most fundamental principles is this: Seeing the truth does not give you the right to pass judgment on it however you wish. Truth is power. And power requires responsibility.

“Truth is seen not by light, but by the eye.”

02

Mythology

Among the seven sisters, Talia is one of those who places the greatest importance on understanding people’s inner worlds.

When a disagreement arises, her first question is not “Who is right?” First she asks “What happened?” Then: “Why did it happen?” And only after that: “What should be done now?”

This order is the foundation of Talia’s character. Because, in her eyes, justice is not as simple as finding and punishing the guilty. For a judgment to be fair, the truth must be understood as fully as possible. This is why Talia has often become the listener to disagreements among her sisters. Yet this does not mean she always seeks compromise.

In some situations, compromise itself can be unjust. Talia knows this.

03

Talia’s Worldview

Talia does not believe most people are bad because they lie. She knows that people sometimes hide the truth out of fear, shame, love, guilt, or to protect someone else. This does not make a lie right. But it can make it understandable. In Talia’s teaching, understanding and forgiveness are not the same thing. Understanding someone does not mean having to accept what they did.

Loving someone does not mean protecting them from consequences, either. This is why Talia’s understanding of justice is in constant tension with compassion. Justice without compassion can become cruel. Compassion without justice can ignore other people’s pain. Talia believes there is no easy answer between the two. Truth is often not easy anyway.

04

Her Bond with Veritasa

In Veritasa’s social structure, truth, justice, and spiritual understanding are not merely religious concepts. They also shape the kingdom’s institutions.

Veritasa’s four great temples are among the centers of the kingdom’s spiritual life. The temples are not merely buildings for worship; they are centers connected with education, thought, records, and social life.

The clergy’s task is not merely to conduct ceremonies, either. Listening to people, understanding disagreements, passing on teachings, and mediating between society and the sacred order when necessary are among their responsibilities. For this reason, a person’s word matters in Veritasa. Yet one person’s word is never accepted as the whole truth.

An event is heard from as many different sides as possible. Because, according to Talia’s teaching: Perspective does not change the truth; but it changes which part of the truth we can see.

05

The Four Temples

Veritasa’s four great temples are not copies of one another. They are considered four centers representing different aspects of the same faith. Yet their shared tasks are clear: They are places where people not only pray to Talia, but learn, discuss, keep records, and confront themselves. In some parts of the temples, records of old decisions and historical events are kept.

Because forgetting the past is seen as dangerous in Veritasa. If a society does not remember its own mistakes, it can repeat the same injustice under another name.

06

Truth and Secrets

Talia is the goddess of secrets, but this does not mean she wants to reveal every secret. Quite the opposite. For Talia, the idea that every truth must be spoken is dangerous, too. Some information belongs to a person. Some, revealed at the wrong time, can harm the innocent. And some must be given only to people who can bear the responsibility of learning it.

For this reason, an important distinction exists in Talia’s teaching: Lies and privacy are not the same thing. A person protecting a truth of their own is not always deception. Yet using a secret to harm someone else, control them, or evade justice is contrary to Talia’s order. This distinction is very important in Veritasa’s culture.

07

Love

It is no coincidence that love is among Talia’s domains. Because, according to Talia, one of the moments when a person struggles most to see the truth is when they must accept the truth about someone they love. Love can make a person understanding. But it can also blind them. For this reason, Talia does not see love only as a romantic or gentle emotion. Loving sometimes requires accepting that someone has done wrong.

Sometimes forgiving. Sometimes not forgiving. Sometimes staying beside them. And sometimes leaving. In Talia’s teaching, love’s value is preserved as long as it does not lead a person away from the truth.

08

Talia in the Eyes of Her People

The people of Veritasa often do not ask Talia to prove them right. At least, in the ideal of the teaching, they should not. There is an older prayer spoken when turning to Talia: “Show me not what I want to see, but what I need to see.” Someone caught in a disagreement may turn to her to be able to make the right decision. Before an important judgment, a ruler may pray to be able to notice their own prejudice.

A person may want to learn whether something a loved one told them is true. Yet Talia’s teaching offers a warning here that might be considered merciless: Before asking for the truth, think about what you will do with it. Because a truth once learned cannot be made unknown again.

09

Justice

Talia’s justice is not revenge. She believes that when punishment’s only purpose is to inflict pain, it can cease to be justice. Yet this does not mean Talia opposes punishment, either. Some actions must have consequences. Some boundaries must be preserved. Some people must be stopped in a way that prevents them from harming others again. Talia’s compassion does not center the perpetrator’s story while ignoring the victim’s pain.

Understanding both sides does not mean accepting that both are equally right. This distinction is one of the most important parts of her teaching.

10

Talia Among Her Sisters

Talia is patient about listening to what her sisters say. Yet being goddesses does not place them above truth in Talia’s eyes. When one of her sisters makes a mistake, she does not ignore it. Loving them does not change this, either. For this reason, Talia may sometimes be the one who says things her sisters do not want to hear. Nora sees what might happen.

Yuna considers the balance of consequences. In the middle of all this, Talia asks another question: “What truly happened?” Although these three perspectives seem close, they are different. Nora concerns herself with possibility, Yuna with balance, and Talia with truth and judgment.

11

Character

Talia is calm and graceful, but not passive. She prefers waiting to forcing people to speak. Silence does not disturb her. She can listen without interrupting while the person opposite her defends themselves. This quality sometimes creates a false sense of security in people. Because Talia understanding you does not mean she agrees with you. One of her strongest qualities is being able to separate emotional understanding from rational judgment.

Yet Talia’s own weakness emerges here, too. Seeking another cause beneath every truth can sometimes make deciding difficult. At some point, one must pass judgment rather than keep trying to understand. And this is exactly the responsibility Talia bears.

“Truth is seen not by light, but by the eye.”

THE FOUR TEMPLES

The four centers of Veritasa’s spiritual and intellectual life. Alongside worship, they are also used for education, records, and collective memory.

sacred place where people are believed to come not to prove themselves right, but to confront their own truths.

03

THE CLERGY

The clergy carrying Talia’s teaching are not responsible only for religious ceremonies. Listening, teaching, and helping disagreements to be understood are part of their tasks.

04

RECORDS

Recording past decisions and important events is close to a sacred responsibility in Veritasa. Forgotten injustice is believed to repeat itself.

05

TALIA’S STATUE

In most depictions, Talia is shown not as a victorious ruler, but as a figure listening carefully or turning her eyes toward the person before her.

06

DAILY LIFE IN VERITASA

Talia’s teaching does not live only in temples. From education to public discussion, the idea of reaching accurate information and listening to different sides is part of society.

07

JUDGMENT A

decision being made quickly does not mean it is right. In Veritasa’s tradition, listening before judgment is not weakness, but responsibility.
